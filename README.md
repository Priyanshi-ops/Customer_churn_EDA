Objective:
Analyze factors influencing customer churn, focusing on contract types, payment methods, tenure, and demographics.

Key Insights:

Contract Type: Month-to-month customers have the highest churn (42%), while one-year and two-year contracts show 11% and 3% churn respectively. Longer contracts improve retention.

Payment Methods: Customers using electronic checks churn at 45%, much higher than credit cards or bank transfers (~15-18%).

Tenure: Churn is highest in the first year (50%), drops to 35% for 1–3 years, and 15% after three years. Early engagement is crucial.

Internet Service: Fiber optic users churn more (30%) than DSL users (20%).

Senior Citizens: Churn rate is 41% vs 26% for non-seniors, suggesting targeted retention efforts.

Visual Insights:
Bar charts and line graphs highlight higher churn among electronic check users, short-tenure customers, and month-to-month contracts, emphasizing the need for early engagement and personalized retention strategies.
